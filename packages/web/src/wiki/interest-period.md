# Interest period and record day

A bond pays interest for **periods**: e.g. six months from 11 January to 11 July. Each period has three dates that matter:

| Date | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Start** | Interest starts accruing again from zero. |
| **Record day** (*dzień ustalenia prawa*) | Whoever holds the bond at the end of this day, after settlement, gets the coupon. Usually several session days before the payment day; the terms set it. |
| **Payment day** | The coupon is paid, for the whole period. |

What a purchase today gets depends on where its **settlement day** (T+2, see [Accrued interest and settlement](#/wiki?page=accrued-interest)) falls in the period, not the day you place the order.

## The period bar

Catalyst's **Period** column and the bond's **Coupons** tab show the current period as a bar: from the start to the payment day, with a tick at the record day and filled up to the day a purchase today would settle.

- **Amber, before the record day.** You pay the accrued interest so far and get the whole coupon. On an ordinary account the broker taxes that coupon in full, so 19% of the accrued interest you paid is tax paid early: it comes back only at maturity, as a cost, if there's a gain to set it off ([Tax on coupons](#/wiki?page=coupon-tax)). The longer the bar, the more accrued interest and the more tax paid early.
- **Green, after the record day.** No accrued interest; the coming coupon goes to the seller. The bond trades without it, and your interest starts with the next period.

Sorting by Period puts the least accrued interest first: a bond just past the record day, or just after the period's start.

## What it means for buying

- **The bar isn't a bargain sign.** Buying right before the record day doesn't earn a coupon for free: you pay for it in accrued interest. And bond prices don't drop by the coupon after the record day the way a share drops after a dividend: the clean price doesn't contain the interest.
- **What a long amber bar costs** is the tax on the accrued interest, paid early: e.g. 392 zł accrued → about 74 zł of tax months or years before it can come back, or never if the bond doesn't end with a gain.
- **On IKE and IKZE** the bar costs nothing: no tax is withheld on coupons there.
- A short amber bar (a few days or weeks into the period) costs little. If you're choosing between days, buying just after the record day or early in the period is the cleanest.
